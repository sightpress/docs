Roles and permissions¶

Everyone who signs in to a SightPress website has one role. A role is a set of permissions: each permission lets a person do one thing, such as publish pages. Each website keeps its own people and roles, so someone can be an editor on one website and a contributor on another.

The four roles¶

Every new website has these roles. The admin panel describes each one in the same words when you invite someone or change their role.

Role What the admin panel says In short
Admin Can do everything, including inviting people, changing roles and site settings. Runs the website.
Editor Can write, review and publish any page, and invite contributors. Looks after the content.
Contributor Can write pages and send them for review, but not publish them. Writes.
Member Can sign in and read the site, but not use the admin panel. Reads.

The admin and member roles can't be renamed or deleted. The member role never has any permission. Every new website has one admin: the person named when it was created (see Create a website).

What each role can do¶

Permission What it lets you do Admin Editor Contributor Member

Edit own pages Change pages they wrote. yes yes yes no
Edit any page Change pages anyone wrote. yes yes no no
Submit pages for review Send a page to the editors. yes yes yes no
Publish pages Approve, publish and unpublish pages. yes yes no no
Archive pages Take pages off the site and keep them. yes yes no no
Delete pages Delete pages for good. yes yes no no
Upload media Add pictures, video, audio and files. yes yes yes no
Publish media Make uploaded files public. yes yes no no
Manage menus Change the main and footer menus. yes yes no no
Invite people Invite people to the site with a role they are allowed to give. yes yes no no
Manage users Change the role of any user, and deactivate accounts. yes no no no
Manage roles Create roles and change what they allow. yes no no no
Manage site settings Change the site's name, profile, branding and other settings. yes no no no
Manage features Turn the site's optional features on and off, and delete their data (how). yes no no no
Apply themes Choose the site's theme. yes no no no
View the audit log See who changed what, and when. yes no no no

Archiving pages, deleting pages, more themes and editing roles in the admin panel come in later versions; the permissions already exist so that roles are ready for them.

Pages and roles¶

Every page goes through the same steps before visitors can see it. Editors' own pages go through review too.

Step From To Who can take it
Submit for review Draft Waiting for review The person who wrote the page, or anyone who can edit any page
Send back to draft Waiting for review Draft Editors and admins
Approve Waiting for review Approved Editors and admins
Publish Approved Published Editors and admins
Unpublish Published Draft Editors and admins

Only published pages are on the public website. When a page is submitted, every editor and admin (except the person who submitted it) gets an e-mail about it. Contributors see their own pages and published pages in the admin panel, and can't change a page while it waits for review.

Who can invite whom¶

  • Admins can invite someone with any role.
  • Editors can invite contributors only. Their form shows just that role.
  • Contributors and members can't invite anyone.

The invited person gets an e-mail with a 6-digit code, never a link. See Codes we send you.

Change someone's role¶

Admins do this in the admin panel:

  1. Choose People in the admin menu. You should see a table of everyone with an account.
  2. Choose the person's name.
  3. Under Change role, choose the new role, then choose Change role. You should hear or see, for example, "Cleo’s role is now Editor." The new role applies from the person's next page.

On the same page, Deactivate account stops someone signing in straight away; their pages stay. Reactivate lets them sign in again with their old password. Nobody can change their own role or deactivate themselves, so a website always keeps someone who can manage people.

Custom roles¶

Admins (or anyone whose role may Manage roles) make their own roles on the Roles screen: a name, then the permissions to tick, grouped by area, each with what it allows. Editor and Contributor can be changed too. Admin always has every permission and Member never has one, so those two can't be changed. Permissions of features that are off aren't listed, and roles keep them. Nobody can take Manage roles away from their own role, and a role that someone has can't be deleted. Step by step: Create a custom role.

For developers¶

Permission names, for modules and the command line: admin.access, page.create, page.edit.own, page.edit.any, page.submit, page.publish, page.archive, page.delete, media.upload, media.publish, menu.manage, user.invite, user.manage, role.manage, settings.manage, module.manage, theme.apply and audit.view.

  • A role that has any permission also has admin.access.
  • A module adds permissions with $registrar->permissions()->add(...). They are copied into every website and given to their default roles, and always to the admin role.
  • The role people get when they register themselves (site setting registration_role, default member) may only hold permissions that are safe for strangers: admin.access, page.create, page.edit.own, page.submit and media.upload. Self-registration arrives in a later version.
